    About

    Afro-Colombian writer, scholar and advocate

    Yesenia Escobar Espitia is an Afro-Colombian writer, poet, professor and lawyer, born and raised in Barranquilla, Colombia, and now based in Philadelphia. Her work moves between literature, education, law and cultural advocacy, with a common thread: the lives, histories and voices of Black communities in Colombia, Brazil and the Caribbean.

    She holds a bachelor’s degree in Modern Languages (Spanish–English) from Universidad del Atlántico, a master’s in Literary Studies from Universidad Nacional de Colombia, and a Juris Doctor from Universidad La Gran Colombia, where she specialised in human rights. She is a Ph.D. candidate in Spanish at Temple University, writing a dissertation on ancestral-affect in Afro-Latin American women writers from Colombia, Brazil and the Hispanic Caribbean.

    Her academic work has appeared in edited volumes including AfroLatinas and LatiNegras (Lexington Books, 2022) and Africology and the African Diaspora (Lexington/Bloomsbury), and she has presented at LASA, BRASA, NeMLA and MLA. In 2024 she received the CLA Research Award for Advanced Doctoral Students at Temple, which supported research travel to Brazil, Colombia and Cuba.

    As a poet, she is the author of Anoche soñé con cocodrilos (FIPPR, 2025) and Ensueño negro del África mía (2016), and of the children’s books ¿Dónde estás, Masmelo? (2019) and Mamá Avó (2014). Her poems and stories have appeared in anthologies published in Colombia and Italy, and she has been invited to poetry festivals in Colombia, Puerto Rico and the United States.

    For more than twenty years she has taught languages, literature and writing at universities in Colombia and the United States. She is an honorary member of Red Elegguá and Director of International Affairs at INABRA, the National Afro-Brazilian Institute, and she has worked as a consultant on the implementation of Afro-Colombian studies in Bogotá’s public schools.
