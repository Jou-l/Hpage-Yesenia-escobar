    Profiles

    Yesenia Escobar Espitia works across four fields: literature, law, education and culture. Explore each profile below.

    Profile 01 · Writing

    Author & Poet

    Yesenia is the author of two poetry collections and two children’s books. Her writing centres the lives of Black women in Colombia and the Caribbean, working through ancestry, memory and the everyday, and she has been invited to poetry festivals in Colombia, Puerto Rico and the United States.

    • Anoche soñé con cocodrilos (2025)
    • Ensueño negro del África mía (2016)
    • ¿Dónde estás, Masmelo? (2019)
    • Mamá Avó (2014)

    Profile 02 · Law

    Lawyer & Human Rights Researcher

    A lawyer with a Juris Doctor from Universidad La Gran Colombia and a specialisation in human rights, Yesenia’s legal work focuses on migration, gender and Afro-descendant communities. Her thesis examined the migration policies of Colombia and Chile (2007–2017) and the protection of Afro-Colombian migrant women. In 2018 she advised the Bogotá Department of Education on implementing the Cátedra de Estudios Afrocolombianos.

    • Human rights and migration
    • Afro-Colombian and Afro-diasporic communities
    • Gender and law
    • Public policy and education

    Profile 03 · Education

    Spanish Teacher & Professor

    With more than twenty years in education, Yesenia has taught Spanish, English and literature at universities in Colombia and the United States. At Temple University she taught Spanish Basic I–III and worked on curriculum development and program assessment; she has also taught at UNAD, Universidad Minuto de Dios and Universidad La Gran Colombia.

    • Spanish language and literature
    • English as a foreign language
    • Curriculum development
    • Afro-descendant studies in the classroom

    Profile 04 · Culture

    Afro Culture Advocate

    Yesenia is an honorary member of Red Elegguá, a Colombian NGO that promotes anti-racist education through the study of Africa and the African diaspora, and Director of International Affairs at INABRA, the National Afro-Brazilian Institute. Her work connects scholarship, poetry and community organising across Colombia, Brazil and the United States.

    • Talks and workshops on Afro-descendant heritage
    • Afro-Latin American literature and thought
    • Cultural events and poetry festivals
    • Advocacy and representation
